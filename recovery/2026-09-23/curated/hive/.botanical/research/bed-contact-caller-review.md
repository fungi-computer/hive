## Ordering determination

**An Upper floor painting over the foot of an Upper bed does not, by itself, require splitting the bed sprite.** `construction-view.js:162–167` gives both Upper sites `x + z + .35 + .2`. `construction.js:68–79` makes a bed occupy its anchor plus the next +x or +z cell. That second supporting floor therefore sorts one full unit after the bed and can paint over its foot. Both supporting floors should precede the bed body. A full-footprint precedence relation can express that while retaining one whole bed sprite.

Do not fix it by using `max(footprint x+z)` plus another constant and claim generalized occlusion: that may move the bed incorrectly in front of nearby actors or other structures. Represent the floor-before-supported-body relation in the bounded ordering contract, then evaluate actual actor/bed/cover relations. Split real geometry only if those constraints require interleaving or form a cycle. The current source still has one Sprite per site and no render-parts module; this is a contract, not a claim parts are already landed.

`src/art/home.js:96–108` places the bed along local +z: board center z=.5 and length1.88; its upper blanket surface is approximately y=.31. Direction1 rotates this whole geometry to +x. `view.js:398–426` always renders the actor at `visualPosition(person)` and selects its pose by activity/cargo, without a bed contact transform. `art/figures.js:694` rotates human sleep geometry separately. Root should review the resulting actual sleep composition; simply using ground feet at the bed anchor does not establish mattress/pillow contact. A sleep-specific view anchor/pose is separate from support-floor order.

Using only anchor neighbors would reject usable side access near the foot. Do not confuse a usable interaction slot with the broad ordinary nav graph: a bed may be unwalkable to passing actors while the assigned sleeper can occupy its own authored contact pose.

Contact survives the requesting task's removal until the body actually exits. Follow that policy rather than leaving actor x/z at an outside anchor while merely drawing the body elsewhere. No second persisted visual position should be invented.

## Wake, interruption and Draft are the migration's most important edge

- `activity.ts:50–65 finishActivity/interruptWork` clears mode/task/path/assignment but does not move the actor. `rest:293–301` finishes through that path. If the actor's logical cell is still inside the bed, waking leaves an idle standing actor on newly blocked furniture.
- `routine.ts:18–27` ends night tasks directly with `finishActivity`, including a sleeper and a walker heading to a bed. Any slot release or exit rule must cover this path, not only explicit rest completion. Routine jobs are then removed; an exit obligation cannot rely on that deleted job continuing to exist.
- `orders.ts:341–347` Draft calls `interruptWork` and immediately sets drafted=true. A paused Draft is already admitted without advancing time. Do not make a paused Draft teleport the actor to an arbitrary free tile or advance an exit animation/tick. If physical use-slot location is adopted, decide the representation of a drafted occupant who still needs to leave; current persistence forbids a drafted actor retaining an ordinary task/assignment/cargo/claim.
- `orders.ts:140–150 cancelJob` and direct-order prioritization also interrupt. Slot ownership must be released exactly once, without consuming/removing an unrelated durable rest order. An interruption during approach should not be treated as somebody physically lying in the bed yet.
- `activity.ts:225–232` wakes sleepers during bed/cover deconstruction, before replacing `state.sites`; it also wakes beds that lose shelter. With a physical use slot, derive a valid exit/disposition while the old bed/access information still exists. If no legal exit exists, choose an explicit waiting rule before deconstruct completion rather than deleting the supporting contact first. Removing cover is distinct from removing the occupied bed.
- `movement.js:route` rejects a blocked destination but currently permits a blocked start. That might let a woken actor eventually route out of a newly blocked bed, but it does not make the interim idle/Draft state or its rendering sound. Do not accidentally rely on this permissive start as the entire contact design.

The access/exit cell may not be built over or reassigned in a way that seals the occupant without a supported recovery behavior; dynamic use reservations and edit preflight enforce that without changing permanent terrain topology every tick. When access becomes invalid through a geometry edit, completion must respect the occupant's obligation rather than strand it. `updateRoutine`, `assignWork` and its cargo-continuation branch must exclude an actor with a pending physical exit even if it is taskless/idle, and `advanceDrafted` must sequence Go after that exit. `clearing.ts:step` currently calls routine, then work and drafted movement; the joined contact owner must ensure at most one movement/contact edge advances per actor per tick and no sleep gain occurs after wake has been requested.

## Cargo and save migration

Normal assignment already prioritizes cargo delivery and excludes cargo/herb claims from ordinary rest selection (`jobs.ts:473–513`). `interruptWork` drops wood and carried herbs at the actor's current logical cell (`activity.ts:31–64` and `resources.ts` caller). A new interaction anchor therefore changes where interrupted cargo is dropped if an old saved actor stands inside a bed. Preserve the same goods ID/quantity/claim disposition; do not move the actor first and accidentally drop cargo in a new cell without an explicit migration decision. Completion of a new blocking bed must also account for loose wood; bed placement currently has the special pile/current/path guard only for walls (`construction.js:390–410`).

`persistence.ts:1006–1032` checks actor path edges and upper support, not general actor-vs-bed collision. `checkActorTaskTarget:1277–1290` confirms sleep has a personal rest job and a finished bed but does not validate an access point, exact sleep location, shelter, or unique bed claimant. Thus valid current saves can contain an idle actor on a bed cell; runtime wake naturally produces that. They can also retain paths through beds, including current builders standing on the foot of an unfinished bed. These must not become corrupt saves after a topology-only-looking patch.

Any newly persisted use/access data or stricter bed occupancy semantics needs an explicit schema/migration disposition. Preserve raw old saves and the existing no-load-rewrite rule. Migrate in-memory old sleep approaches/occupants consistently with the selected contact policy, preserving durable rest orders and physical goods. Do not silently reset the clearing, discard cargo or reject all former sleeper/wake states. If a cramped old layout has no outside access cell, that is a real compatibility case requiring a deliberate recovery/egress policy, not a nearest-free-tile teleport hidden inside validation.

A new relational check must reject duplicate live bed-slot claims, mismatched approach/contact phase and invalid access/reference combinations. Current runtime `bedFree` makes duplicate assignment unlikely, but persistence should validate its durable equivalent once the invariant is adopted. This does not require adding an independently mutable `bed.occupied` field. Progress validation is phase-specific: newly started or blocked entry/exit may legitimately have zero progress; only traversal phases have a fixed duration bound. A using/sleep contact is not bounded by one walk-edge duration.

Include a legacy walker whose `leg > 0` and next path cell enters or crosses the bed. Preserving only integer x/z while clearing its path/leg changes the interpolated body position. Root's migration-only egress policy must also preserve or deliberately resolve that already-active edge, with its exact old furniture exception, rather than quietly snapping the body during paused decode. Future unused path segments can be invalidated and replanned separately.
