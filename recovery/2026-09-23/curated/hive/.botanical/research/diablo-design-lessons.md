# Diablo design lessons for Hive

This is an inspiration study, not a proposal to expand the current candidate. Facts below distinguish **Diablo (1996)**, **Diablo II (2000)**, and later **Diablo II: Resurrected** material. The Hive conclusions are labelled inference.

## What the sources establish

- **Diablo (1996):** the official manual separates backpack items from a small belt. Small usable items can be put on the belt, get number hotkeys, and are automatically placed there when picked up if space remains; it then directs the player to meet Tristram’s residents for tasks. That makes readiness, carrying, and the town relationship visible in play. [Original Diablo manual](https://ftp.blizzard.com/pub/misc/Diablo.PDF)
- **Diablo II (2000):** the official manual preserves the backpack/belt distinction: the belt gives quick access to drinkable potions and Town Portal/Identify scrolls, and later belts expand that ready capacity. [Original Diablo II manual](https://download.blizzard.com/pub/misc/Diablo%20II%20Manual.pdf)
- **Diablo II: Resurrected is not original Diablo II.** Blizzard calls its larger stash and automatic gold pickup quality-of-life changes, so neither should be presented as a classic-game constraint or copied as such. [Blizzard’s 2021 remaster announcement](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/23679610/diablo-ii-resurrectedtm-the-gates-of-hell-open-september-23)
- **Combination depth is a later, bounded example, not evidence of a Hive crafting system.** Blizzard’s D2R 2.4 notes show named rune/cube ingredient combinations upgrading a specific set weapon or armour, and explain that the aim was more build possibilities from lower-level set items. This is a modern remaster fact, useful for the pattern “recognisable inputs → named transformation → particular new option.” [Blizzard 2.4 notes](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/23788293/diablo-ii-resurrected-patch-2-4-ladder-now-live)
- **Combat readability came from short action paths.** In a developer account of Diablo’s making, David Brevik describes the move to real-time as immediate movement and attack, and says the late hotbar replaced a lone potion slot. This is a reported developer account, not a rule that Hive needs an action-RPG hotbar. [Game Developer’s GDC report](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/20-years-later-david-brevik-shares-the-story-of-making-i-diablo-i-)

## Two decision loops to borrow, not clone

1. **Prepare → push into danger → turn back with a story and material.** In Diablo’s manuals, ready belt capacity differs from backpack capacity; in D2 it also holds the immediate way home. **Inference for Hive:** let a future party choose a compact ready kit and equipped tool before entering a procedural goblin cave/dungeon entrance during colony downtime. Risk, capacity, injury, and what was found decide when to return; home then provides rest, sorting, repair, and a visible account of the trip. Returned resources become inputs to building and crafting rather than vendor trash. This is the safe-settlement/dangerous-expedition rhythm without pretending a colony is a solo dungeon.

2. **Find a distinct thing → decide its place → make a concrete good.** Diablo makes location meaningful (belt versus backpack); D2R’s named recipes demonstrate that combination value can be legible without every object becoming generic currency. **Inference for Hive:** a future charm, tool, relic, seed, or magical material should first answer where it belongs—equipped by whom, carried for this trip, stored at home, installed at a workshop, or consumed in a named ritual. A small authored combination can open one capability, alter one familiar’s work, or yield gear a particular adventurer/shop actually wants, with inputs and result visible before commitment. That supports player role-play specialisation through a resource and demand loop rather than a universal crafting catalogue.

## Three transferable decisions

1. **Give future items a visible job and location.** Show equipped, ready/carried, colony-stored, and installed as separate relations in UI and world state. The authorized spatial inventory can use rotation and capacity as real expedition choices when each item’s footprint, carried state, and equipment effect remain legible. The D1/D2 belt is the useful precedent: a small readiness boundary changes choices alongside a deeper pack.

2. **Make a dangerous command read before and after it lands.** For a future spell, trap, weapon, or expedition order, selection should name the actor/tool/target, preview its reach or cost where relevant, and leave an observable result in the world or log. This adapts Diablo’s direct movement/use cadence to colony commands; it does not require real-time click combat.

3. **Let home convert danger into care, production, and demand.** Use the return point for recovery, sorting a limited number of finds, a workshop/building decision, and a changed relationship or next lead. Let visiting or resident adventurers make legible gear requests that selected player shops can meet. It gives cozy-dark play relief between tense excursions and lets acquired things matter as materials, crafted gear, and social/economic promises rather than sale value.

**Fit warning:** the authorized deep semi-procedural gear/crafting and spatial inventory should not turn into excess junk, repetitive sorting clicks, illegible stacks, or drops that make crafted goods irrelevant. Protect the meaningful distinctions between equipped, carried, stored, and installed; give crafted goods and shop demand durable reasons to matter beside found gear. This remains a future direction, not a claim that random affixes, loot tables, or spatial inventory are implemented now.
