# Isometric terrain: shape, exposed edges and readable volume

Bounded read-only study, 2026-09-08. No runtime, production asset, dependency, proof or engine change. These are source-supported mechanisms and proposed Hive study criteria, not implemented features or measured performance.

Primary implementations inspected: OpenRCT2 develop at `092783cc1160be24f2acbed023217558bdb1f41a`; OpenTTD master at `de306de89347fb2435028d25b72a831551d3607c`; Tiled stable documentation identifies itself as 1.12.2. Both repository pins are development snapshots. No source or artwork is proposed for copying.

Hive context: `src/art/scale.js` retains the accepted 32×16 ground diamond, orthographic Three camera and 2.16 geometry-unit storey. The existing vertical, generation/streaming and geometric-ordering decisions already separate voxel address, physical height, standable surface, rendering and persistence. This study strengthens those boundaries; it does not select a voxel height or enlarge the playable clearing.

## 1. Generation outputs legal terrain geometry; isometric projection comes later

OpenTTD generates a temporary height field, normalizes water/coasts/slopes, then transfers heights into map records. Its slope model encodes raised corners and special steep/half-tile cases. The slope smoother exists specifically because arbitrary generated height differences do not fit its terrain representation. OpenRCT2 likewise separates heightmap creation, conversion to surface records, beaches and tree placement. [OpenTTD generation](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/blob/de306de89347fb2435028d25b72a831551d3607c/src/tgp.cpp#L884-L938), [OpenTTD slope vocabulary](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/blob/de306de89347fb2435028d25b72a831551d3607c/src/slope_type.h#L36-L89), [OpenRCT2 generation](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2/blob/092783cc1160be24f2acbed023217558bdb1f41a/src/openrct2/world/map_generator/MapGen.cpp#L25-L49)

**Hive application:** macro geography and local noise produce canonical materials/volume and explicit surface shapes. A shape classifier selects flat, ramp, ledge and cliff geometry under a versioned rule. Projection merely displays that result. Do not smooth away all cliffs to accommodate an underspecified renderer. Conversely, do not draw a ramp over a full blocking cube and call the cell walkable: a physical ramp needs matching support, clearance and height queries. Surface heightfields remain useful for broad geography; one height per horizontal cell cannot encode an overhang or a cave with multiple surfaces.

## 2. Exposed cliff sides are independent of the top texture

OpenRCT2's surface record separates slope, surface style, edge style and water height. `ViewportSurfaceDrawTileSideBottom` compares **both endpoint heights** of an edge with the corresponding neighboring edge. It skips a fully hidden edge, otherwise emits vertical strips and triangular fillers between the neighbor and its own slope. Tunnel openings have separate pieces and bounds within that edge construction. Equal neighboring water heights suppress their shared side; this is rendering behavior, not a finite-water simulation. [Surface record](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2/blob/092783cc1160be24f2acbed023217558bdb1f41a/src/openrct2/world/tile_element/SurfaceElement.h), [exposed-edge implementation](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2/blob/092783cc1160be24f2acbed023217558bdb1f41a/src/openrct2/paint/tile_element/Paint.Surface.cpp#L448-L638)

**Hive application:** derive top faces, visible side intervals, corners, slope caps and openings from canonical neighboring geometry. For block terrain, omit solid-to-solid interior faces; for ramps, compare actual shared edge profiles. Material transitions and exposed solid faces are separate decisions. A grass cap can sit over a soil/rock cliff without making every vertical face green. Bake original Three terrain parts through the accepted camera: restrained top texture, distinct lit/shaded side palettes, a readable turf lip, and sparse strata/details. Keep direction-specific lighting; flipping a lit bitmap is not equivalent to rebaking the opposite orientation.

## 3. Transition masks solve seams without making a giant atlas of whole scenes

Tiled's terrain sets encode edge, corner or mixed adjacency constraints. A complete two-terrain edge or corner set has 16 patterns; mixed combinations grow substantially. Its brush updates neighboring tiles and can choose weighted variations of equivalent patterns. The same mechanism supports isometric tiles, but does not edit multiple layers together. [Tiled terrain documentation](https://docs.mapeditor.org/en/stable/manual/terrain/)

**Hive application:** use a small surface transition vocabulary for grass/soil, bank/water and rock/soil. Derive masks from material neighbors, independently of the slope/volume descriptor. Cosmetic variation uses stable global coordinates and an art-specific seed, so revisiting a chunk does not rearrange its surface. Do not generate every combination of biome × moisture × slope × cliff height × season as a unique sprite. Start with reusable base shape and bounded compatible overlays; apply the planned lighting/palette treatment consistently. Treat the atlas as a presentation dictionary, not as the authority on soil, water or navigation.

## 4. Cutaway must expose geometry, not merely hide the topmost sprite

OpenRCT2 has explicit clip-height/rectangle tests. When a neighbor is clipped away, its exposed-edge caller treats that neighbor as absent for drawing the boundary. Underground mode selects different edge presentation; base and vertical-side visibility have distinct controls. The renderer therefore adapts what is exposed to the view policy. This does not establish a general volumetric cave simulation in OpenRCT2. [Clip and edge handling](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2/blob/092783cc1160be24f2acbed023217558bdb1f41a/src/openrct2/paint/tile_element/Paint.Surface.cpp#L431-L536), [surface visibility caller](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2/blob/092783cc1160be24f2acbed023217558bdb1f41a/src/openrct2/paint/tile_element/Paint.Surface.cpp#L1200-L1258)

**Hive application:** terrain display consumes the existing selected-surface/cutaway policy. At an underground slice, generate the newly visible interior walls and a deliberate cut cap for solids crossing the slice; preserve a clear distinction between genuine openings and a diagnostic cut. Hidden terrain still owns collision, support and environmental boundaries. Picking uses the same visible-part policy and resolves a face to its actual surface/volume target. A click on a cliff face must not silently select the projected ground behind it. Keep utility X-ray, selected-level cutaway and fog-of-war as distinct presentation/knowledge policies.

## 5. Cache terrain parts without flattening away required depth relations

OpenRCT2's edge caller submits separate strips/fillers with world-space bounds, rather than one giant map image. That is useful evidence for retaining the geometry behind an isometric sprite. It is not proof that its ordering algorithm solves every Hive overlap. Hive's accepted geometric partial-order contract still owns interleaving, full actor-pose bounds and explicit split decisions. [Bounded edge submissions](https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2/blob/092783cc1160be24f2acbed023217558bdb1f41a/src/openrct2/paint/tile_element/Paint.Surface.cpp#L620-L638), [Hive ordering decision](../../docs/decisions/isometric-order-and-structural-support.md)

**Proposed caching discipline, not a claim about either engine's chunk implementation:** derive/cache exposed-face descriptors by terrain revision and neighbor dependencies, then select visible parts by camera and cutaway. Cache baked shape textures independently of placement. Compositing several terrain parts into a texture is safe only where no actor, prop or other terrain part needs to appear between those parts. A whole raised chunk as one Pixi sprite generally fails that condition. Use depth-compatible runs/parts and retained geometry instead of abandoning chunk caching or forcing every block into one global all-pairs sort.

A local edge/corner classifier needs its immediate neighbor halo; longer-range smoothing, shadows or ecology must declare their own larger dependency. Edits at chunk boundaries invalidate both affected sides. Unknown/unloaded neighbors are not empty air: retain the needed halo, defer the dependent boundary, or display a clearly provisional boundary. Camera movement can change culling without regenerating terrain. Track CPU descriptors, uploaded textures and cache bytes separately; no performance numbers are claimed here.

## 6. Walkable surfaces and sprite positions need one geometry source

OpenTTD exposes world-space slope height queries and a two-endpoint edge-height query. Its height lookup distinguishes ground from the height taken by a ground vehicle through special terrain such as a bridge/tunnel. This is a useful example of geometry-aware height access rather than sprite-anchor arithmetic; it is not Hive's complete movement solution. [Surface and edge-height queries](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/blob/de306de89347fb2435028d25b72a831551d3607c/src/landscape.cpp#L306-L394)

**Hive application:** terrain geometry provides supported regions, local height samples and explicit neighboring connections to the existing navigation owner. A cliff supplies no ordinary walk edge; a ramp has an authored continuous surface, rise and clearance requirement; a cave ceiling is not its floor. Actors and carried goods derive contact position from the accepted surface/traversal. Slope decoration cannot invent an edge, and removing a rendered cap cannot open a path. This follows the retained `SurfaceRef`/profile/provider contract and its versioned vertical metric; do not add a second terrain-specific pathfinder.

## First original visual study: useful acceptance criteria

Stage an isolated isometric study alongside the current world lab, without moving gameplay into it. Root retains original art review. A bounded region with a neighbor halo and one visible chunk boundary is enough; do not begin with a continent or a long gameplay proof.

1. **Readable geometry at native and game scale:** include flat ground, each ramp direction, a concave/convex bank, a one-step and multi-step cliff, a narrow ledge, a pond rim, and an entrance under an overhang. Use clearly labeled experimental height units until the excavation metric is chosen. Keep Rowan/Sedge and existing props at their accepted scale.
2. **Visible volume:** show grass cap, soil band and rock face meeting without transparent cracks, dark doubled edges or repeating full-block seams. A tunnel entrance must remain legible without a UI outline. Include a flat adjacent control sample to judge whether extra texture is noise.
3. **Occlusion and contact:** place an existing character beside, behind, below and above a cliff/ledge. Include a carried prop and a bed/floor near terrain. Reverse insertion order and cross the problematic depth boundaries. Any required split must recombine without seams or double painting.
4. **Cutaway and picking:** switch surface/underground views over the same records. The slice exposes actual walls/caps; hidden objects do not steal input. Picking reports the intended surface/face and coordinates. Show empty cave floor separately from unknown space.
5. **Seams and bounded derived work:** move across the chunk boundary, edit one boundary cell in the lab, evict/rebuild its presentation and revisit it. Neighbor faces/masks agree and stable cosmetic choices return. Instrument regenerated cells/faces, uploads and retained bytes, distinguishing cold generation from warm camera motion. This study establishes no live digging, water flow or navigation claim until those callers are actually joined and checked.

The first study should answer whether Minecraft-like volume is readable in Hive's existing visual language. It should not decide that every terrain surface must look like a Minecraft cube, nor replace the already planned water, caves, navigation, persistence or chunk-authority contracts.
