## Supported design lessons

- **Start with watershed, route, and access.** The inspected OSU transcript has
  Millison say poorly placed roads can drain water, cause erosion, and degrade
  soil, while well-placed routes can harvest water and promote infiltration.
  For Hive, height, channel route, culvert/gate position, and maintenance may
  change an observed local result; “plant trees” alone is not the mechanic.
- **Slow/spread/infiltrate is a site hypothesis, not magic.** The video pages
  and transcript support rainwater-harvesting structures in seasonal drylands.
  They do not establish one amount of recharge, a universal half-moon geometry,
  a cure for every desert, or a result independent of soil, geology, rainfall,
  vegetation, evaporation, and use.
- **A water budget remains necessary.** Input rain/runoff, storage, infiltration,
  evaporation/transpiration, permitted withdrawal, overflow, and downstream
  flow must have named boundaries. A home cistern, terrace, wetland, channel, or
  spring cannot satisfy unlimited crops, people, baths, and downstream users.
- **Regreening is social and ecological.** Millison's account includes grasses,
  native acacia, fodder, and food security in particular Sahel contexts. Hive
  can use visible maintenance, cultivation, and allocation; it should not claim
  a real community's recovery result or turn restoration into a generic tree
  bonus.

## Groundwater and wetland limits

The [USGS wetland hydrology review](https://water.usgs.gov/nwsum/WSP2425/hydrology.html)
states that wetlands may recharge groundwater, receive groundwater discharge, or
do both; the direction and magnitude vary with topographic position, hydrogeology,
soil/sediment, season, evapotranspiration, and climate. It says most wetlands are
primarily discharge areas, while recharge also occurs.

The [USGS Mojave recharge study](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/pp1703/g/) adds a
useful precision limit: infiltration below the root zone is not automatically
groundwater recharge; water must traverse the unsaturated zone to the water
table, and sediment texture, streamflow, and stratigraphy matter. Therefore:

- holding visible water does **not** inherently create groundwater;
- a wetland must not be labelled a recharge feature merely because it stores
  water, nor a discharge feature merely because it is low or wet;
- a later Hive fixture can expose local storage/moisture/overflow, but cannot
  claim aquifer recharge without a named, measured model and consumer.

## Hive consequence

The later hillside channel/terrace/cistern fixture should retain finite source,
height, route, gate, maintenance, downstream allocation, and overflow facts.
It may show one local bed becoming wetter after a lawful diversion and another
remaining dry. It should not represent continental groundwater, prove that a
wetland is a recharge basin, or make stored water reappear after save/load.
