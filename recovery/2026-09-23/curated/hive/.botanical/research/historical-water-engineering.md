# Historical water engineering: four distinct references

Future direction from Levi, 2026-09-07. Ancient Americas, Hawaiʻi, and Roman
water works can inform original Hive home, garden, hygiene, and technology
choices. They do not form one interchangeable “ancient irrigation” system, nor
do they authorize a hydraulic engine, new physics/source implementation, or a
change to upstairs → brewing delivery order.

Related reviewed notes: [manual hygiene](hygiene-wells-and-bathing.md),
[shallow buried pipes and X-ray](world-voxels-and-utility-view.md), and
[ONI systems follow-up](oni-systems-followup.md).

## Evidence boundary

- The Maya case rests on a 2020 peer-reviewed archaeological study of reservoir
  sediments, not a general account of all Maya water practice.
- The Xochimilco case uses FAO's present GIAHS account of living chinampera
  farming. It does not call the knowledge extinct or attribute every raised
  field in Mesoamerica to the Mexica.
- The Hawaiian case uses University of Hawaiʻi and Hawaiʻi Sea Grant resources
  about ʻauwai and loʻi kalo. These are living cultural practices and histories,
  not a vanished generic “Pacific system.”
- The Roman case uses Frontinus's late-first-century CE Rome water-management
  account and a University of Virginia research resource. It is not evidence
  that every Roman settlement, or every aqueduct, had identical supply.

## Primary and institutional sources

   in Guatemala](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33093562/)”, *Scientific
   Reports* (2020), DOI 10.1038/s41598-020-75023-7. Its abstract supplies the
   Corriental location, cultural period, sediment evidence, and date range.

   It describes present Xochimilco wetland raised fields, canals, crop diversity,

3. Hawaiʻi Sea Grant, “[Ola I Ka Wai: Water is Life](https://seagrant.soest.hawaii.edu/ola-i-ka-wai-water-is-life/)”.
   It describes ʻauwai diversion, rules that protect downstream users, and
   maintenance-linked proportionate shares.
4. University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa, “[Ka Papa Loʻi ʻo Kānewai history](https://manoa.hawaii.edu/hshk/ka-papa-loi-o-kanewai/history/)”.
   It documents an ʻauwai predating the monarchy period, its 1980s rediscovery,
   restoration, and clearing work at a specific Mānoa site.
5. Sextus Julius Frontinus, “[*De aquaeductu urbis Romae*](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Frontinus/De_Aquis/Rodgers/1%2A%2A.html)”,
   scholarly translation hosted by the University of Chicago; and Katherine

   University of Virginia Institute for Advanced Technology in the Humanities.

## Historical lessons, kept separate

### Storage and clean supply

Tikal is the strongest reference for a **reservoir/clean-water** distinction.
The excavated evidence supports a filtered drinking-water reservoir at one site;
it does not establish farm irrigation, sewers, or a specific flow solver. Hive
can learn that source quality, stored amount, filter upkeep, and use destination
are legible separate facts.

### Wet cultivation, channels, and drainage

FAO's Xochimilco description supports canals as part of a living raised-field
agricultural landscape: beds, channel edges, aquatic vegetation, transport, and
seasonal regulation work together. The game lesson is not “water makes crops
grow.” It is that a garden’s geometry can create moisture, drainage, access, and
maintenance choices. A canal or terrace must not silently count as a potable
water source.

### Gravity, allocation, and downstream rights

Hawaiʻi Sea Grant describes allocation rules limiting an ʻauwai diversion to
half the stream's flow, protecting downstream users, and linking maintenance
to growers' shares. That is a source-specific historical account, not a claim
about present water law or every site and period. Hive should carry forward
the readable idea of an upstream diversion affecting someone/something below;
it should not pretend to model Hawaiian law or turn people/cultures into a
generic faction bonus.

### Conveyance, nodes, upkeep, and use tiers

Frontinus records elevation, routes, delivery tanks, allotment, legal rights,
and maintenance in Rome; Rinne describes gravity flow and later constrained
distribution. This supports visible channel/cistern/branch choices and periodic
inspection. It does not require pipes to wait exclusively for “Dwarven” tech:
simple gravity channels, ceramic/wooden conduits, and cisterns can be early
authored construction; Dwarven discovery can later add pumps, durable pressure
controls, treatment, or more reliable distribution.

## One bounded future fixture: hillside garden watercourse

After the active home loop, propose one authored fixture on a small slope:

1. A finite spring/cistern source sits one elevation above two short terrace beds.
   A player chooses one wooden gate: route the named allocation through a lined
   channel to the upper bed, or bypass it into a lower cistern.
2. An open channel has one capacity and a visible downstream end. Water that
   fills the upper bed can be released through one drain to the lower cistern;
   a closed gate leaves the downstream destination unserved. The fixture records
   explicit input, stored amount, allowed route, output, and overflow/loss.
3. One **clear silt / mend leak** ordinary work order restores channel capacity.
   One **open/close gate** order changes the actual route. A person carrying a
   vessel may draw from the cistern for a basin, but no new player bucket puzzle
   is required.
4. A small readout shows source amount, gate state, upstream and downstream
   moisture, cistern amount, channel condition, and the reason a bed is dry.
   Pause/save/reload preserves the allocation, water, pending work, and any
   carried vessel exactly once.

This is a gravity/channel/terrace/cistern proof, not a generic fluid engine.
Its behavior may be an authored fixture configuration with a named slope and
finite transfer rule. Actual source quantities, routes, gates and capacity must
cause the transfers; a decorative channel plus a scripted watering timer would
not prove the system. It does not choose cell resolution, pressure equations,
aquifer generation, long-distance hydraulics, or full erosion.

## Ownership, hygiene, and jobs

- One future resource/environment owner holds source, stored water, channel
  condition, route/gate state, cistern, vessel, and transfer claims. Rendering
  reads these facts; a channel sprite never creates water.
- The hygiene study's well → vessel → basin and latrine → waste cycle may consume
  the cistern’s named clean-water amount. Potable source, irrigation route, and
  waste destination remain distinct; waste never becomes crop fertility or clean
  water without a later explicit consumer and process.
- Clear, mend, fill, carry, and drain are ordinary work with reachable positions,
  claims, cancellation, pause, and save semantics. A later actor-local wash or
  relief activity reserves its fixture; it does not create a competing water
  owner or interrupt Draft/Go outside an explicit safe boundary.
- Sol's ONI heat/smoke direction remains separate. This fixture may later publish
  a named water/condition input, but it does not create a second temperature,
  gas, weather, or smoke model.
