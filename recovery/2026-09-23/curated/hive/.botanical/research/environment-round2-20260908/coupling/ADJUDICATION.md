## Water occupies air space

`Vgas = Vvoid − Vwater` is a derived geometry/occupancy relation. A fixed-reference-density extension requires integrated outward carrier volume to equal the lost gas volume; instantaneous continuity becomes `Σ q_out = −dVgas/dt`. The existing gas solver enforces fixed-volume divergence zero and cannot accept that edit. Shared accounting must reject or request a new solve, never clip water and air independently.

The [independent tank fixture](coupling_fixture.py) adds 0.25 m³ water through prescribed motion into a tank with an ideal open exhaust. Gas free volume falls 0.8→0.55 m³; it exports **0.30 kg reference air, 2.5 g tracer and 3,015 J thermal anomaly**. The remaining concentration and air temperature stay unchanged. Closed or unknown exhaust rejects atomically. Negative thermal anomaly remains valid. This is a deliberately separate, well-mixed, zero-backpressure displacement approximation, **not a changing-volume capability of the sibling gas solver**. [Results](independent-results-run2.json).

[One actual water receipt](physical-water-air-bound-results.json) also drives that extension: the solver's accepted 0.603602 L receiving-cell increase over a bound 50 ms interval exports the same reference-air volume. Old/new water volumes, integrated faces, geometry revision and exact interval agree. The ordinary fixed-volume gas adapter rejects that same intrusion. This is a physical-output coupling fixture, not a globally joined water/gas evolution loop or finite-exhaust pressure solve.

The sealed counterexample instead retains ideal-gas mass. Prescribed reversible adiabatic compression from 0.8→0.55 m³ raises pressure **101.325→171.194 kPa**, temperature **303.15→352.13 K**, and requires **32.765 kJ external work**. An unpressurized water model cannot impose that displacement without feedback. These are analytic ideal-gas relations, not calibrated Hive parameters. [NASA derivation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/compression-and-expansion/).

For the separate constant-pressure/isothermal vented ideal-gas example, internal energy changes −63.372 kJ, enthalpy export is 88.704 kJ and moving-boundary work is +25.331 kJ. Exporting internal energy while also adding that work invents 25.331 kJ. NIST's control-volume equation distinguishes internal energy, enthalpy flow and moving-volume work. [NIST TN1299, §2.1, printed p.13](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=910125).

## Thermal and numerical limits remain material

Air and water keep separate thermal capacities. In the approximate tank, capacity-weighted equilibrium is **287.600 K**; averaging their temperatures instead gives 295.372 K and invents **14.625 MJ** of the modeled sensible thermal quantity. This anomaly model is not compressible gas internal energy.

Wet excavation lowers represented water gravitational potential by **24.360 kJ**, while history reset and hydraulic friction dissipate motion without a thermal/work ledger. Those are explicit hydraulic approximations. Volume conservation plus balanced sensible heat cannot be promoted to conservation of total mechanical/thermal energy. A future coupling must either account these conversions or state the excluded terms and its permitted consumers.
