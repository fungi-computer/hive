# Homestead compost heat and poultry-adjacent greenhouse source check

Read-only source check, 2026-09-07. Levi’s future direction is crops over active
compost/hotbeds, compost-assisted greenhouse warmth, and a chicken coop beside a
greenhouse with useful heat but honest ventilation and moisture tradeoffs. This
does not add a current runtime feature, give real-world husbandry instructions,
or select a heat/air solver.

## Sources inspected

1. Purdue Extension, “[Hotbeds and Cold Frames](https://ag.purdue.edu/department/hla/extension/extension-publications-library/ext-pubs/ho-53-w.html)”.
   Its manure-heated pit hotbed has drainage material, moistened manure, then a
   3–4 inch planting-soil layer; it calls for soil-temperature measurement before
   seeding.
2. Penn State Extension, “[Two Designs for Low Cost Hot Beds](https://extension.psu.edu/two-designs-for-low-cost-hot-beds-for-small-scale-production)”.
   Its farm-informed composting-manure hot box uses manure plus bedding/carbon,
   water and turning, a cover, and a wait for fresh-manure ammonia to dissipate
   before transplants are placed.
3. Missouri Extension, “[Building and Using Hotbeds and Cold Frames](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g6965)”.
   It describes manure heat as microbial decomposition, notes large input volume,
   drainage dependence, difficult temperature control, a soil layer over manure,
   and a limited seasonal use.
4. Beck-Friis et al., “[Advanced experimental composting reactor](https://doi.org/10.1006/jaer.2000.0661)”,
   _Journal of Agricultural Engineering Research_ (2001). The insulated,
   instrumented reactor independently controlled oxygen and temperature; daily
   water/mixing addressed moisture losses from heat and aeration.
5. University of Maryland Extension, “[Coop construction: general requirements](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/coop-construction-general-requirements)”.
   It identifies poultry heat, carbon dioxide, and moisture production, and says
   ventilation supplies oxygen and removes moisture, carbon dioxide, and ammonia.

## What the evidence supports

### Active compost/hotbed

- **Layered root separation is real.** Purdue and Missouri describe manure below
  a distinct several-inch soil/planting layer; Purdue includes drainage material
  beneath. Penn State's hot box similarly uses a landscape-fabric cover over the
  active compost before transplants. That supports an explicit active-compost
  layer and separate root medium, rather than growing roots directly in a generic
  “hot compost” state.
- **Heat is generated by decomposition and is conditional.** Missouri attributes
  manure heat to microbial activity. Penn State calls out manure/bedding balance,
  water, and turning; its example can fail to heat when bedding is excessive.
  The reactor study directly demonstrates temperature, oxygen, gas flow, and
  water content as measured variables, rather than a permanent heat buff.
- **Heat is finite and can be wrong for plants.** Missouri says manure-heated
  temperature control is difficult and use is short-season; it advises waiting
  for soil temperature to fall before planting and ventilating when too warm.
  Purdue likewise calls for thermometer checks. Penn State warns fresh-manure
  ammonia can harm small plants before it dissipates.
- **Compost has service needs and an end state.** A pile may cool, dry, become
  too wet, lose available feedstock, or need turning/aeration. After active heat
  is exhausted, it can become a different mature compost/soil-amendment resource;
  this source check does not specify a crop-yield formula or real chemistry.

### Coop next to greenhouse

- **Useful heat is plausible, sufficiency is unproven here.** Maryland documents
  that poultry add heat to coop air. No inspected source establishes that a small
  adjacent coop will maintain a greenhouse at a required crop temperature, nor
  gives a safe universal heat-transfer design. Treat any gained warmth as a
  measured/visible local effect, not a guaranteed all-night heater.
- **Do not seal or recirculate shared air by default.** Maryland states that coop
  ventilation is needed to bring oxygen and remove moisture, carbon dioxide, and
  ammonia. A greenhouse needs its own ventilation/moisture decisions. A shared
  wall may be a future authored heat boundary; a direct coop-to-greenhouse air
  path is not justified by these sources.
- **Waste and bedding are materials, not atmosphere.** Poultry bedding/manure can
  become a named compost input only through an explicit collection/compost job.
  It must not make crop air, clean water, or fertility appear automatically.

## Conservative Hive proposal

The first later proof can use one **compost hotbed** and no full greenhouse or
animal simulation:

1. A worker combines one finite brown/bedding input, one green/manure input, and
   water in a hotbed. The bed records feedstock, moisture band, aeration state,
   biological activity and available decomposition energy, and mature-compost output separately. Heat generation debits that process budget into the shared environmental heat owner; it does not maintain a second room-temperature state.
2. The build has four visible layers: drainage/base, active compost, a distinct
   root-medium layer, and one crop. The crop reads only local root temperature
   and moisture, not the raw compost inventory.
3. **Turn/aerate**, **water bed**, and **add feedstock** are ordinary worker jobs
   with material claims. Their result can change the conditions governing decomposition;
   no continuous chemistry or generic compost controller is implied.
4. The player sees too-cold, too-hot, dry, wet, or exhausted reasons and can open
   a vent/shade action where a covered frame exists. One finite active phase ends
   in mature compost; it never provides free permanent heat.

A later **coop-adjacent greenhouse** can reuse the same boundaries: the coop has
a local heat/moisture/waste output, the greenhouse has its own crop conditions,
and a wall/duct configuration has a declared heat transfer. It must separately
declare fresh-air exchange and moisture removal; no animal air is silently sent
into the grow room. A player can then compare a vented wall, closed wall, or
maintained heat transfer, without implementing a whole building-HVAC system.

## Ownership and sequencing

- The future environmental owner holds heat, moisture/air-quality quantities and
  opening connectivity. Compost, coop, greenhouse, crop, and rendered particles
  read it; none owns a duplicate room climate. This remains compatible with the
  separate ONI smoke/heat direction without superseding it.
- World/resources own feedstock, water, active/mature compost, waste, and claims.
  Construction provides reachable work/use positions; ordinary jobs retain pause,
  cancellation, Draft/Go safe-interruption, and save rules.
- Associate the future proof with [issue 10](https://github.com/fungi-computer/hive/issues/10)
  for finite water, [issue 14](https://github.com/fungi-computer/hive/issues/14) for crop
  conditions and recipes, and the hygiene/water direction for named manure/waste
  custody. It does not displace upstairs → brewing or choose a full ecology,
  livestock-health, greenhouse, plumbing, or ventilation framework.

The portable lesson is a short, inspectable chain: feedstock and water → managed
active compost → bounded root-zone warmth → crop condition → mature compost.
Coop adjacency can later add a measured heat/waste tradeoff, but clean air and
ventilation remain independent requirements.
